Sahih (authentic) صحيح

A hadith that meets all five conditions (Ibn al-Salah's definition):

  1. Ittisal al-sanad: continuous chain; every narrator heard directly from the one before.
  2. Adalah: every narrator is upright (Muslim, mature, sane, free of open sin and of conduct that compromises integrity).
  3. Dabt tamm: every narrator has full precision, in memory or in written record.
  4. No shudhudh: it does not contradict the narration of more reliable narrators.
  5. No illah: it has no hidden defect that undermines it.

Hasan (good) حسن

Meets the same conditions as Sahih, except that one or more narrators have lighter precision (khafif al-dabt), though they remain upright. It is a rank just below Sahih.

Types

TypeMeaning
Sahih li-dhatihiAuthentic in itself, meeting all five conditions.
Sahih li-ghayrihiHasan li-dhatihi raised to Sahih by supporting chains.
Hasan li-dhatihiGood in itself, with lighter precision of narrators.
Hasan li-ghayrihiMildly weak hadith (not due to a liar or open sinner) strengthened by other chains.

Ruling

Both Sahih and Hasan are accepted as evidence (hujjah) for beliefs and rulings. Sahih ranks higher, which matters when the two conflict.

Eligibility in this collection

  • Include: Sahih li-dhatihi, Sahih li-ghayrihi, Hasan li-dhatihi, Hasan li-ghayrihi.
  • Exclude: Da'if (weak), Munkar, Matruk, Mawdu' (fabricated).
  • Bukhari and Muslim: treated as Sahih by the scholars' consensus on the books as a whole; the other books and Mishkat require a grading (e.g., Albani, Shuayb al-Arnaut).
  • Tirmidhi's "Hasan Sahih" is a technical phrase whose meaning scholars discuss; record the grade you adopt and its source.

Graders sometimes differ on a single hadith; always note the grader.
